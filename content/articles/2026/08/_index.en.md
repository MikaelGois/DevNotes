August is the eighth month of the year in the Julian and Gregorian calendars, lasting 31 days. August owes its name to the Roman emperor Augustus (*Octavianus*), being renamed in his honor by decision of the Roman Senate in 8 BC. Originally called *Sextilis* (from the Latin *sextus*, "sixth", as it was the sixth month of the ancient Roman calendar). Julius Caesar established that the year should begin on the first new moon after the winter solstice, which in the Northern Hemisphere was December 21st, starting in the Roman year 709 (45 BC). At that time, the beginning of the year occurred eight days after the solstice. Later, the beginning of the year was changed to eleven days after the solstice.

It is on average one of the coldest months in most of the Southern Hemisphere, where it marks the peak of winter, and one of the hottest months in much of the Northern Hemisphere, where it marks the peak of summer. In the Southern Hemisphere, August is the seasonal equivalent of February in the Northern Hemisphere, and vice versa.
